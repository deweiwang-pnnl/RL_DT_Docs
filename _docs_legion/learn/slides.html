Part 2

The gripper is a four-bar linkage — and the lift blocker lived there

  • One driven joint; the rest follow through mimic and loop-closing joints.
  • We drove a loop joint to 0 (URDF habit) → pads in a V.
  • Pads travel 18 mm toward the table while closing → grasp height.
  • Close across the 85 mm side, not 128 mm.
  • Contact offset / depenetration decide whether fingers sink in — Isaac Lab's 2F-140 values fixed it.
  • Never test a grasp by teleporting the object: it overrides the solver.
Same scripted grasp: 09-20 setup (left) vs fixed (right). 16 / 16 after the fix.

Part 2

Gravity compensation: what a real UR controller does

Implicit PD drives are springs toward a target. With gravity and no compensation, the spring needs an error to hold the arm up — and relative IK starts the next step from the sagged pose.

Fix: every physics step add G(q) — the torques that hold the robot at configuration q — from PhysX inverse dynamics, as a feed-forward effort.

ArmIK error after 300 steps
gravity off0.0 mm
gravity on434 mm
gravity on + G(q)0.0 mm

Consequence: policies trained with gravity off transferred unchanged (E4 54.7 %). Code: mdp/gravity_comp.py.

Part 3

PPO — four details that decided whole stages

1 · Input normaliser
(x − mean) / (std + 0.01). An input that never varied has std ≈ 0 → a new value is huge. A 180°-flipped plate (same plate!) became a ~100σ input. Canonicalise symmetric quantities.
2 · Rewards × dt
Each term is weight × value × 1/30 s. A bonus of weight 50 pays 1.7.
3 · Truncation is not termination
Success is a truncation: the future value is bootstrapped, so the bonus is the only gain from finishing. 1.7 < critic noise ±5–7 → "placed but held". Weight 600 → 1.6 % → 67 %.
4 · Learning-rate schedule
rsl_rl's KL-adaptive rate (÷1.5 per minibatch when KL > 2× target) sat at its 1e-5 floor → fixed 1e-4. But: it did not explain the E5 library gap — one finding is not a rule.

Also: actions are clipped to ±1, so a wide action std is "free" — it grew 1.0 → 3.7 and randomly opened the binary gripper → entropy 0.01 → 0.001.

V(s′) ≈ V(s) and rhold ≈ rrelease, so the preference for releasing is ≈ the bonus. PPO only sees it if it stands out from the advantage noise.

Part 4

Measure the policy you deploy, not the training curve

  • Blue: training success with noise — often falls within a stage.
  • Orange: deterministic evaluation, 128 episodes (σ ≈ 4.4 points at 50 %) — every gate.
  • Look at per-episode final states (--diag_stack): "placed but held", "never picked up", "released askew".
  • Probe the setup before training (grasp gate, multi-plate checks, arm tracking).

Why not one policy?
Training both placements together overwrote A (swap test) and then taught "don't grasp" — every carry toward the stack crashed.
Why start states?
B trained from the home pose; at the real hand-off its first move dragged the plate A had just placed.
Dense damage penalties
Success already requires "lower plate in place", but only at the end — a displacement penalty says so at once.
Open
One policy that chains both placements itself — the natural next research step.

Part 6

Libraries: same task, same env steps, one evaluation

  • Budget in env steps, not iterations (an iteration means different data per library).
  • One shared deterministic evaluation (eval_common.py).
  • Only skrl PPO learned Align from scratch — reason open; next test: value-target normalisation.
  • SB3 / SAC settings were under-powered; not a verdict.
  • One seed each: a direction, not a variance.
